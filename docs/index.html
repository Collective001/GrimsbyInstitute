The Institute

A learned society most comfortable in the margins.

Founded in 1862, the Institute was established to preserve experimental medical records judged too provisional for ordinary publication and too carefully witnessed to discard. Its work has always been custodial.

The H-31 sequence remains the most requested collection. It begins as a clinical record and becomes a travel narrative, then something more difficult to classify without improving it falsely.

The Archive

Journals of Dr. Elias Grimsby

Titles are Dr. Grimsby's own. The Institute has preserved missing leaves, contradictory dates, and unexplained procedures as part of the record rather than correcting them into confidence.

Reading Room

Conditions for consultation

Readers are asked to proceed in sequence and to avoid summarising a volume before opening the next. Personal theories may be recorded on the loose cream slips provided, but must not be inserted into the bindings.

The Institute cannot confirm private reconstructions of H-31 without the corresponding absence slips.
